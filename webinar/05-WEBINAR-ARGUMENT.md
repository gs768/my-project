# Webinar argument structure: "Google told us. Google's spam updates confirmed it. Here's what happened."

**Logic chain:** (1) What Google *says* ranks LSA → (2) what Google *punishes* (spam updates) → (3) the strategy that satisfies both → (4) proof from three client profiles.
**Why it works:** each claim is Google's own words on screen. The audience reaches the conclusion before you state it, and the case studies close the argument. Nothing rests on "trust me."

**Hook (open with this):** "Every agency's 'secret sauce' for LSA is citations and photos. Google never says that. Let me show you what Google does say."

---

## Act 1: What Google says helps LSA (≈10 min)
Screenshot the official sources, highlight with a box/underline, and show the URL bar plus capture date on every one.

| Slide | Screenshot | Highlight | Your one-line interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | LSA Help Center: "how ads are ranked" page | The factor list: proximity, review score and count, **responsiveness to calls/messages**, business hours, serious/repeated complaints | "Five of these are things you control. Citations and photos aren't on the list." |

| 1.5 | Google Ads Help: Quality Score components (expected CTR, ad relevance, **landing page experience**) | Landing page experience | "Your Search Ads run next to LSA, and your website decides that bill." |

## Act 2: What Google punishes (≈10 min)
| Slide | Screenshot | Highlight | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Google Search Central: spam policies, **Doorway pages** | "multiple city/region pages that funnel users" example | "The cheap 'car accident lawyer + every city' pages most agencies sell are named here." |
| 2.2 | Search Central blog: March 2024 core/spam update, **scaled content abuse** | "content at scale… to manipulate rankings" | "AI city-swap pages are now a liability, not an asset." |
| 2.3 | Spam policies: site reputation abuse / expired domain abuse | Headline definitions | "Buying old domains and 'parasite' pages stopped working too." |
| 2.4 | GBP / Maps content policy: fake engagement & review rules | Incentivized, gated or bulk reviews | "Batch review blasts after a settlement get filtered, and that's your LSA review score." |
| 2.5 | GBP guidelines: keyword stuffing in names, address rules | The business-name rule | "Shortcuts here risk suspension, and LSA is tied to the profile." |

**Turn (one slide, black background):** *"So Google rewards local relevance, responsiveness and real reviews, and it punishes fake scale. Very few firms do both."*

## Act 3: The strategy (≈15 min) (show what and why, hold back how)
1. **Hub-and-Spoke Local Authority, the version that survives spam updates.** One hub per metro, plus spokes only where you have *real local proof* (case results, courthouse, crash corridors, attorney ties, local reviews). Fewer, better pages. Ties to 1.5 (landing page / Quality Score) and avoids 2.1–2.2.
2. **Responsiveness Engine.** Answer rate, speed to lead, after-hours coverage, missed-call text-back. Ties to 1.1–1.2.
3. **Review velocity that doesn't trip the filter.** One-by-one text requests spread over time, never batched. Ties to 1.4 and 2.4.
4. **Weekly dispute routine.** Ties to 1.3.
Show the diagram and a scorecard. Don't show templates, link maps, call flows or scripts.

## Act 4: Proof: three profile pieces (≈15 min)
Same format for each firm so the pattern is unmistakable: **Before → What we implemented (which of the 4) → After.**

| Firm | Screenshots to capture (before & after, same date span) |
|---|---|
| **Obral Silk & Pal (OSP)** | LSA dashboard: leads/month, cost per lead, charged vs. credited leads, review count/score |
| **Langley Law Firm** | Same, plus a BrightLocal Local Search Grid before/after for the core keyword |
| **Meyer Gutierrez** | Same, plus CallRail answer rate/response time before/after |

- Put one headline number per firm on the slide (e.g., "Cost per lead −X%", "Leads +Y%"). Details stay in the screenshot.
- Each one closes with a client quote or video clip.
- **Before using any of it:** written permission from each firm, blur client/caller names and phone numbers, no guaranteed-results language, and footnote "Results vary; past results don't guarantee future outcomes."

## Close (≈5 min)
- Recap the chain on one slide: Google says → Google punishes → strategy → results.
- "3 things you can do this week": dispute last month's bad leads, check your answer rate, stop batch review requests.
- Call to action: **LSA Market Audit** (Meeting 1), limited slots, $20 ticket credited.
